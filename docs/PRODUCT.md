# Noma

Noma shows a person the data trail their phone produces. Android first; evidence before narrative. M0 delivers a local chronological view of app resume/pause events and DNS requests with source-backed organisation classifications. Unknown is preferable to false attribution. No generated/sample events in the live view.

First run: promise → explain app access and DNS coverage → opt in separately → inspect timeline/evidence → pause or erase. M0 does not claim the full Your Day acceptance test: successful connections, reliable originating apps, long-day collection and battery cost must still be validated. Play My Day, location, LLM and removal requests are later milestones.

M1 adds an Organisations tab over a local Personal Data Graph. It groups recorded DNS names by the bundled dataset's owner and categories. Each name leads to its original request evidence; unknown names are visible separately. Category labels are source classifications, never claims that a company collected particular information. M1 is developed at the user's request while M0 compatibility remains open.

M2 makes Your Day the opening view. Today/Yesterday show a factual summary generated entirely on the device, without a language model at the user's request. Calendar dates use the current device timezone. Every narrative statement links to its supporting records and classification sources. Empty days say no records are available, not that the person did nothing. Partial retention and collection gaps stay visible.

M3 adds Play My Day as the primary action above the summary. A dark green, full-screen story uses large timestamps, progressive counts, short fades and a final recap. Controls remain visible while long content scrolls. It starts only with real retained records and uses no illustrative OTR activity or invented travel. Chapters group nearby records but explicitly keep app activity separate from DNS. Every frame exposes its original evidence; inspecting pauses playback. Pause, back/next, skip, replay and close give the user control. Seven-second pacing is a presentation interval, not elapsed real-world time. Playback pauses when the activity stops and starts paused with touch exploration enabled.

M4's Take Back tab provides privacy-request preparation, official-page navigation and local response tracking. All three public entries remain available without observations and are labelled accordingly. Users choose access, deletion, objection, consent withdrawal or advertising opt-out, then edit a draft. They submit independently through an appropriate official route and record dates/references locally. Completion is user-reported, never independently verified deletion. Drafts/history survive the short activity-retention window but remain covered by Erase my data. Missing contacts and region-specific mechanisms are shown as unverified rather than invented.
